Big pole barn: van bay (Airstream Interstate 24X)

  • (a) Cheapest: winterize the van and/or keep it on shore power. The 24X has 12 V heat pads on the fresh and gray tanks that switch on at 44°F and off at 64°F; the black tank is warmed by the furnace (2022 Interstate 24X owner's manual). The Timberline system's coolant needs no winterizing, but the domestic water must be drained or filled with RV antifreeze (Airstream Support). The barn already has a 30 A shore outlet; check it's on its own circuit at the barn subpanel. Cost: about $0–300.
  • (b) Recommended: insulate just the van bay and add a thermostat heater set to 40°F. Closed-cell foam on the large section (~2,000 sf, about $3.6–7K) plus a Modine Hot Dawg HD45 propane power-vented heater, $963–1,429 (Amazon, SupplyHouse), plus gas line, vent and install (estimate). About $6–11K (optional line). An electric unit heater on a 240 V circuit is the no-combustion alternative.
  • (c) Insulate the whole barn (~32 × 38): a 30×40 pole barn runs $2.6–13.5K (HomeGuide), plus the heater. About $5–16K.
  • Safety: propane heaters must be vented outside (power-vent or direct-vent; no unvented heaters near a parked vehicle). Put a CO alarm in the barn and keep propane cylinders outside.

  • Its own mini-split zone, separate from the propane furnace

Zoning & code: addition or second unit?

  • The Whitewater ordinance doesn't define "dwelling unit" or "kitchen." A two-family dwelling is one "occupied exclusively by two families living independently of each other," and multiple-family uses "independently doing their own cooking" as the test S
  • Michigan Residential Code 2021: a dwelling unit means "complete independent living facilities … including permanent provisions for living, sleeping, eating, cooking and sanitation." A kitchen is "an area used, or designated to be used, for the preparation of food" (MRC ch. 2) S
  • Read: with no permanent cooking appliance, and plug-ins only, there's a reasonable case that it's an addition to the single-family home, needing no fire separation and no duplex review. But a separate entrance, lockable door, bath and sink look like a unit, and the "designated for food preparation" wording cuts the other way. Get a written determination from the township Zoning Administrator (231-267-5141) and GT County Construction Codes (231-995-6044) before designing I
  • If ruled a second unit: A-1 allows two-family dwellings by right, with plot-plan review (§12.12). The building code then requires a 1-hour dwelling-unit separation (MRC R302.3) from foundation to roof sheathing, a rated self-closing connecting door per the official, separate smoke and CO alarms, and possibly separate utilities. The ban on rentals under 30 days still applies S/I

Site conflicts

  • Propane tank (position inferred): sits inside the footprint, so it must move. NFPA 58: ≥10 ft from buildings for 125–500 gal, 25 ft for 501–2,000 gal, and fill and relief connections ≥10 ft from openings and ignition sources. The model moves it about 19 ft west of the suite. The supplier does the move, roughly $500–1,500 I
  • Well (position inferred, about 22 ft north of the footprint): outside it. County Sanitary Code §3.310 requires the well ≥50 ft from the septic tank and field and ≥10 ft from sewer lines. Route the suite drain south. If the real well is inside or within about 10 ft of the footprint, that means a new well (~$8–15K) or this spot is out S/I
  • Septic: adding a bath, a sink and a sleeping room means a GT County Environmental Health capacity review (systems are sized by bedrooms; tank and field ≥10 ft from footings). A 1996 system permitted for 4 bedrooms may need a larger tank or field, roughly $5–25K. The inferred field is about 36 ft from the suite S/I
